## Provenance: `AI`

CONTRIBUTING requires one of `human` / `AI` / `mix`, describing **who wrote the Lean proofs**.
The Lean proofs in *both* routes were largely generated by Aristotle (Harmonic), so `AI`
("mostly produced by an AI system") is the honest value. An earlier draft of this card said
`mix`; that overstated the human share of the proof writing.

The human contribution is real, but it sits beside the proofs rather than in them:

| Route | Lines | Blueprint | Lean proofs | Human work |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Franklin (primary) | 1264 (28%) | **written by the authors** | largely machine-generated, following that blueprint | substantial manual intervention and review, lemma by lemma against the blueprint |
| q-series / JTP | 3318 (72%) | machine-generated | machine-generated | restructuring the library, renaming the API for mathlib-readiness, reviewing statements |

Claude assisted with Lean proof development, blueprint maintenance and CI/deployment
infrastructure throughout.

If a maintainer would rather record this as `mix` on the strength of the author-written Franklin
blueprint and the volume of manual intervention there, we would not argue — but `AI` is the value
we would choose ourselves, and the pool exists partly to host exactly this kind of work. What we
would not do is claim `human`, or leave the split unstated and let a reviewer discover it.

Note that the correctness argument is unaffected: every result is machine-checked. No `sorry`, no
axioms beyond the standard three, and CI fails on any warning from Mathlib's linter set. How a
proof was produced changes nothing about whether the kernel accepts it.

## Above the bar

Two things worth leading with, since they are uncommon among pooled projects:

- **100% docstring coverage** — 220/220 public declarations documented.
- **A blueprint.** Every formalized declaration is tied to a prose statement and paragraph-level
  proof sketch; `leanblueprint checkdecls` enforces in CI that no `\lean{}` annotation names a
  declaration that does not exist. Published with doc-gen4 API docs at
  <https://viazovska.github.io/PentagonalNumberTheorem/>.

## The one thing a reviewer will probe

The repository contains **two independent proofs of the same theorem**. That is a strength for
confidence, but a curator may ask whether both belong in a shared pool. Pre-empt it: the q-series
route is the reusable library (and the part with no Mathlib overlap), while the Franklin route is
a self-contained combinatorial proof that already builds on Mathlib's `Nat.Partition.genFun` and
serves as an independent check. Submitting them as one card, under a single entry module that
imports both, presents this accurately.
